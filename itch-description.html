NOT 1942 DX

by JDC · version DX 1.0

NOT 1942 DX is the deluxe remake of NOT 1942, a vertically scrolling shoot-'em-up for the Commodore 64 inspired by the arcade classic 1942. It keeps the game: four levels, four bosses, the music and the controls. Everything you see is new: smooth scrolling, far more on screen at once, and redrawn levels, planes and bosses. Written from scratch in 6502 assembly.

Fly a twin-boom fighter across four battlegrounds against squadrons that fly in formation:

  • Fighters in V's, echelons and strings that dive, swoop, loop and zig-zag
  • Leaders with wingmen, and gunships that hover and fire aimed shots
  • Raiders that climb up from behind you
  • Dive bombers, and enemy aces in their own fast fighters
  • Red leaders that drop gold medals worth 500 points when shot down

You have three lives, one hit kills, and a short blinking invulnerability after each respawn. A full run takes about eight minutes, if you can survive it.

Levels

Each level scrolls for about a minute and a half over its own landscape, then ends in a big boss fight with a health bar and two attack phases.

  • Level 1: Open Ocean. Tropical islands with rolling surf and drifting clouds, ending with the four-engine bomber Thunder.
  • Level 2: Jungle Coast. A jungle shore with rivers, a village and an enemy airstrip, ending with the armoured airship Leviathan.
  • Level 3: Sunset Strait. A winding strait between sunlit cliffs, past anchored boats, gun pits and a blinking lighthouse, ending with the giant flying boat Albatross.
  • Level 4: Enemy Fleet. Destroyers, cruisers and carriers steaming through a storm-grey sea, their fighters launching as you pass, ending with the enemy flagship Kraken.

Beat all four and you'll see the ending.

What's new in DX

  • Smooth scrolling, one pixel every frame, over the whole screen
  • A sprite multiplexer: up to 18 sprites on screen at once (the original had 8), so squadrons fly in real formations, up to six planes at a time
  • Redrawn levels with animated water and surf, clouds, and in level 4 a sea that moves past the ships
  • New planes, and bigger bosses (up to 144×84 pixels) with longer fights
  • Multi-stage explosions, screen shake, and fades between levels
  • The score and lives float over the playfield, so the whole screen is game
  • Press 1, 2, 3 or 4 on the title screen to practise that level's boss

Features

  • Original 3-voice SID music: a title song, a theme for each level and a boss theme
  • Sound effects for explosions, medal pickups, boss hits and a big blast when a boss goes down, mixed in with the music
  • Pause at any time, and an option to play with sound effects only
  • Runs on a stock PAL C64 at 1 MHz
  • Loads as a single compressed 95-block file, or boots instantly from the cartridge image

Controls

  • Joystick in port 2: move and fire
  • Keyboard: W A S D to move, Space to fire
  • P or RUN/STOP: pause and resume
  • M on the title screen: music on or off
  • 1-4 on the title screen: go straight to that level's boss

MiSTer turbo version

For the MiSTer FPGA's C64 core there is a second version that uses the core's turbo mode for a harder game:

  • More enemies: about 120 to 136 a level instead of 83 to 96, up to seven planes at a time
  • Bosses with bullet patterns: sweeping fans of shots and aimed bursts
  • Room for more enemy fire on screen at once
  • Clouds drifting over everything at twice the speed of the land below, for a real sense of height

It needs the core's Turbo mode set to C128 or Smart and Turbo speed at 2x, 3x or 4x (open the OSD with F12). It checks at start-up: with turbo off it tells you what to set, and starts by itself as soon as turbo is on. The title screen shows the speed it found. It isn't for a real C128, whose graphics chip shows no picture at 2 MHz; it says so and asks for the standard version.

The turbo files are not1942dx-turbo.d64, .prg and .crt. Load the disk with:

LOAD"NOT 1942 DX T",8,1
RUN

How to play

Download the disk image (.d64) and load it on a real C64 or in an emulator such as VICE:

LOAD"NOT 1942 DX",8,1
RUN

On a stock 1541 drive it takes about a minute to load; a fast loader cartridge or JiffyDOS makes that much shorter.

The .prg file is the same game, for emulators that start a program file directly.

The .crt file is a cartridge image (Magic Desk type): attach it in your emulator, or flash it to a cartridge, and the game starts at power-on with no loading.

Needs a PAL machine, or an emulator set to PAL; NTSC isn't supported.
